Measure a conversation
the way the literature says to

Point it at two videos of a conversation — one per person. It works out who spoke when, how quickly each replied, what they looked at, when they nodded and how many times each nod bobbed, how tightly each listener's responses tracked the partner — 203 documented measures, every decision cited, every detector validated before it is trusted.

Behavior, counted the way a coder counts it

Rates hide the facts a human coder writes down. Conversation Analyst reports the count beside every rate, the length of every nod, and who was listening when it happened.

Nods by cycle

Single, double, triple — exact, not impressionistic

Nod lengths follow the cycle definitions of Mori, Den & Jokinen (2025, PLoS ONE). Calibrated against their 9,223 hand-annotated nods: the pipeline reproduces 42.1% single nods against their 42%. Every nod is labelled speaking / listening — a distinction the nod-typology literature is built on.

Responsiveness

Does their responding track the partner, or a clock?

A conditional-intensity model (Hawkes 1971) separates two listeners with identical backchannel rates: baseline rate, responses evoked per partner pause, and the timescale of the coupling — validated by parameter recovery on simulated listeners.

Tempo phases

Where the conversation changed gear

Bayesian online changepoint detection (Adams & MacKay 2007) over exchange tempo, with every boundary forced to win an exact Bayes-factor test. Constant-tempo simulations come back as exactly one phase, ten out of ten.

Turn-taking

Latencies, overlaps, backchannels, repair

Floor transfers centred near the 200 ms the cross-linguistic literature reports (Stivers et al. 2009), backchannels separated from turns, interruptions from projection, hesitations found acoustically because transcripts lose them.

Transcript

Readable, searchable, honest about itself

The full conversation follows playback; uncertain words are underlined; a lab vocabulary fixes names the recognizer has never heard, and every correction is shown with what was originally heard.

Verdicts

PASS means the numbers can be trusted

What a recording cannot support is withheld with a stated reason — overlap on shared audio, movement from a frozen view — and the verdict grades what remains. A dash is never a zero.

Spreadsheets, not a black box

Every number leaves as a CSV, in the shape the question is usually asked in. Nothing has to be reshaped before the first test.

counts.csv

One row per participant, one column per count

How many nods, words, smiles, laughs, questions, backchannels, interruptions and turns — 30 raw counts, no rates, nothing pre-aggregated. Add a column for whatever groups your participants and the comparison is one line of R.

By family

The catalogue split one file per family

Head, gaze, prosody, turn-taking, lexical, affect — 20 files rather than one with 203 measures in it, each in both long and wide form, with an index naming what is in which.

Transcript

What was said, as rows

One row per utterance with speaker, clock time and text — including the backchannels and the attempts that got talked over, which a turn table leaves out. And one row per word, with per-word timing and the recognizer's confidence in it.

# counts.csv, joined to your own grouping variable
d <- merge(read.csv("counts.csv"), read.csv("participants.csv"))

t.test(nod_count ~ group, data = d)
#>  mean in group 1   mean in group 2
#>             30.4              20.1

# ...and again per minute, because conversation length is in the same file
t.test(I(nod_count / duration_min) ~ group, data = d)
An empty cell is never a zero. A count that could not be measured — a camera that froze, a view with no face in it — stays empty, and a companion file states the reason. A participant who did not nod and a participant whose camera failed must not read the same way. Counts that belong to the pair rather than to one person go in their own file instead of being copied onto both rows, so nothing enters a model twice.

Validated five independent ways

A fitted parameter looks exactly as authoritative as a count while having more ways to be silently wrong — so nothing reaches a report until it survives evidence it cannot grade for itself.

What is not claimed: nod-for-nod agreement with a human coder is unmeasured until someone codes sessions in the built-in coding mode — that is why it exists. Criterion correlations at n=16 are directional, not inferential. Sessions whose recordings cannot support a measure report a stated gap, not a guess.

Every decision, with its citation

The documentation of record is generated from the same registry that computes the numbers: what was decided, why, and on whose published evidence — Mori, Den & Jokinen; Hadar; Poggi; McClave; Stivers; Hawkes; Adams & MacKay; Ekman; Kendon. A measure cannot exist without appearing there.

Download

Free and open source (MIT). No GPU needed. Everything runs on your machine — recordings never leave it.

⊞  Download for Windows — v1.1.0 installer macOS / Linux — v1.1.0 (zip)
1
Run the installer. It installs for your user account — no administrator password, and it appears in Add/Remove Programs like anything else. Windows may show a "protected your PC" notice for new software: click More info → Run anyway.
2
Start it from the Start Menu or Desktop. The first launch sets up the analysis engine — about 1.6 GB of scientific libraries, once, with progress shown. Every later launch opens in seconds.
3
Point it at a folder of recordings — two videos per conversation, one per person — and click Analyze.

macOS / Linux: unzip, then run ./launch-conversation-analyst.sh (needs Python 3.10+ with Tk — brew install python python-tk on a Mac). Same one-time setup on first run.
